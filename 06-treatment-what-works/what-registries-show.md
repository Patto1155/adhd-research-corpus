# What do the big registry studies show about medication and real-life outcomes?

> **Short answer:** Large studies using national health and justice records from Sweden and US insurance databases have compared the same people during periods when they were taking ADHD medication and periods when they were not. During medicated periods, rates of criminal convictions, emergency visits for car crashes, substance-related emergencies and injuries were substantially lower. This "within-person" design is much stronger than simply comparing medicated with unmedicated people, but it is still observational, so it suggests rather than proves that medication caused the difference.

## Why the same-person design matters

**The problem with comparing two groups.** People who take ADHD medication differ from people who do not in many ways: how severe their ADHD is, other conditions, family support, money, access to care. This is called confounding, and a specific version, confounding by indication, means the reason someone is prescribed a medicine is itself linked to the outcome. A simple comparison cannot tell whether a difference comes from the medicine or from who gets it.

**Using each person as their own comparison.** A within-person design (also called a within-individual or self-controlled design) follows people over years and compares each person's rate of an outcome in months on medication with their own rate in months off it. Anything that stays fixed for that person, such as their genes, childhood, personality, sex and baseline severity, is automatically cancelled out, even if it was never measured [1][2].

**Why it is still not an experiment.** The design does not cancel things that *change over time* and also affect both medication and outcome. Someone might start medication after a crisis, stop during a chaotic period, or restart when life is calmer. Risk of some events is highest just before treatment starts, which can make medicated periods look better than they are [3]. Pharmacy records also show that a prescription was collected, not that it was taken. Only a randomised trial removes these problems, and trials of this length and size for outcomes like crime or crashes do not exist.

## What the evidence shows

**Crime.** Among 25,656 Swedish people with ADHD followed from 2006 to 2009, the rate of criminal convictions was 32% lower in men and 41% lower in women during medicated periods than during their own unmedicated periods (hazard ratios 0.68 and 0.59) [1]. Across sensitivity analyses in men, the reduction ranged from 17% to 46%. The authors wrote that the findings "raise the possibility" that medication reduces criminality.

**Car crashes.** In US insurance records for 2.3 million adolescents and adults with ADHD, the odds of an emergency visit for a motor vehicle crash were 38% lower for men (odds ratio 0.62) and 42% lower for women (0.58) in months with medication than in the same people's months without [2]. Crashes that did not lead to medical care were not counted. Absolute crash rates per month were not extracted for this page.

**Substance-related emergencies.** In US claims for about 3 million adolescents and adults, emergency visits linked to substance use were 35% lower in men (odds ratio 0.65) and 31% lower in women (0.69) in medicated months. Two years after a medicated period, the odds were still 19% and 14% lower [4]. The authors cautioned against reading this as evidence that ADHD medication treats addiction. The wider picture is on [alcohol, drugs and addiction](../05-what-often-comes-with-it/alcohol-drugs-and-addiction.md).

**Injuries.** A meta-analysis of studies in young people found ADHD associated with about 1.5 times the odds of unintentional injury, and medication associated with about 12% fewer injuries in self-controlled analyses over the short term [5]. A broader 2020 meta-analysis of registry studies found medication associated with fewer accidents and injuries (odds ratio about 0.72), fewer mood disorders (0.69) and fewer poor academic outcomes (0.80); its pooled estimate for criminality was smaller and not statistically significant [6]. <!-- CHECK: Boland 2020 pooled figures (0.72, 0.69, 0.80, criminality HR 0.87 n.s.) taken from a Simply Psychology summary, not the abstract -->

**Suicidal behaviour.** This section discusses suicide in research terms only; support options are listed on [not medical advice](../08-reading-this-well/not-medical-advice.md). In 37,936 Swedish people with ADHD, the rate of suicide-related events was not higher during treatment; within-person it was slightly lower (rate ratio 0.89 overall, 0.81 for stimulants) [7]. A later study found risk tended to be elevated in the period *before* treatment began, which is a warning against reading any rise around the start of treatment as caused by the medicine [3].

**Deaths.** A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis of 19 observational studies reported that people with ADHD had a higher rate of death from any cause than people without ADHD, and that, among people with ADHD, those taking prescribed medication had a lower rate of death from any cause [8]. The medication estimate rested on only three of the 19 studies, and the authors said these observational findings cannot show that medication itself reduces deaths. The figures here come from the university's announcement and trade coverage; the paper's own abstract was not seen for this page, so its estimates are not quoted. Registry findings on medication and mortality, including the absolute numbers needed to interpret them, are on [the hard statistics in context](../04-did-i-cause-this-and-where-is-it-going/the-hard-statistics-in-context.md).

## What is uncertain or debated

Time-varying confounding is the main unresolved weakness, as set out above. Most findings come from Sweden and US commercially insured people, so they may not transfer to other health systems. Absolute rates were often not reported in the abstracts available for this page, so the relative reductions above cannot be turned into "how many events avoided per 1,000 people" here. Some studies were part-funded by drug manufacturers or had authors with industry ties; this does not make them wrong, but readers may reasonably weigh it. <!-- CHECK: funding/conflict details of each cited study not checked individually -->

## What this means for you

These studies are among the strongest real-world evidence that ADHD medication is associated with fewer serious everyday harms, and they hold up in a design that removes many obvious explanations. They describe averages across very large groups and cannot promise any individual outcome, and they say nothing about whether a particular person should take medication; that is a decision made with a prescriber.
