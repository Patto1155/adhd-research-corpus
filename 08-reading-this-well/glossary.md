# ADHD terms explained: what do the technical words mean?

> **Short answer:** This is an A to Z list of the 135 technical terms used on these pages, each explained in one to three sentences, with a link to the page where it matters most. Pages define a term the first time they use it; this list is for when you have forgotten or want to check.

For the ideas behind effect sizes, heritability and risk, with worked examples, see [Reading ADHD research: what do the numbers really mean?](how-to-read-the-evidence.md) The definitions here follow the wording the pages themselves use.

## A

**Absolute risk.** How often something actually happens in a group, for example about 6 in 10,000 people a year. Always read it alongside relative risk. → [Will ADHD shorten my life? The hard statistics in context](../04-did-i-cause-this-and-where-is-it-going/the-hard-statistics-in-context.md)

**Actigraphy.** Measuring sleep and activity with a wrist-worn movement sensor over days or weeks. It is more objective than a sleep diary but less detailed than a sleep-lab study. → [Sleep and ADHD: why can't I fall asleep, and is sleep the cause?](../03-why-i-think-and-feel-this-way/sleep-and-the-body-clock.md)

**ADHD (attention deficit hyperactivity disorder).** A neurodevelopmental condition defined by persistent inattention and/or hyperactivity and impulsivity that start in childhood and cause problems in daily life. "ADD" is an older name, now covered by the mainly inattentive presentation. → [What is ADHD, actually?](../01-is-this-real/what-adhd-is.md)

**ADHD-RS (ADHD Rating Scale).** A standard questionnaire scoring the 18 ADHD symptoms, from 0 to 72 points, used in many medication trials to measure change. → [How well does ADHD medication work?](../06-treatment-what-works/how-well-medication-works.md)

**Adjusted.** A result is adjusted when the analysis has statistically accounted for other factors, such as age or sex, that could explain the link. Adjustment only covers the factors that were measured. → [Reading ADHD research: what do the numbers really mean?](how-to-read-the-evidence.md)

**Alpha-2A agonists (α2A agonists).** Non-stimulant ADHD medicines, guanfacine and clonidine, that stimulate a noradrenaline receptor (the alpha-2A adrenoceptor) in the prefrontal cortex. → [How do ADHD medicines work?](../06-treatment-what-works/how-adhd-medicines-work.md)

**Amphetamines.** A family of stimulant medicines (including dexamfetamine and lisdexamfetamine) that block the transporters for dopamine and noradrenaline and also push extra of both out of nerve cells. → [How do ADHD medicines work?](../06-treatment-what-works/how-adhd-medicines-work.md)

**Amygdala.** A small structure deep in each side of the brain, involved in emotion. In the largest scan study it was very slightly smaller on average in ADHD (effect size −0.19), with about 92% overlap between groups. → [Does ADHD mean my brain is damaged or "three years behind"?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/brain-structure-and-development.md)

**ASRS (Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale).** A short questionnaire, in its screening form six questions, used to flag adults who may have ADHD. A high score suggests an assessment is worthwhile; it is not a diagnosis. → [How is ADHD diagnosed, and why is there no test?](../01-is-this-real/how-diagnosis-works.md)

**Association.** Two things occurring together more often than chance would predict. An association does not show that one causes the other. → [Reading ADHD research: what do the numbers really mean?](how-to-read-the-evidence.md)

**Atomoxetine.** A non-stimulant medicine that mainly blocks the noradrenaline transporter. It usually takes weeks rather than hours to reach its full effect. → [How do ADHD medicines work?](../06-treatment-what-works/how-adhd-medicines-work.md)

**Autism (autism spectrum disorder).** A neurodevelopmental condition involving differences in social communication and repetitive behaviours or interests. It often co-occurs with ADHD, and both can be diagnosed in the same person. → [Autism and ADHD: can you have both?](../05-what-often-comes-with-it/autism-and-adhd.md)

## B

**Biomarker.** A measurable sign in the body, such as a scan, blood or brain-wave result, that indicates a condition. No biomarker can diagnose ADHD. → [How is ADHD diagnosed, and why is there no test?](../01-is-this-real/how-diagnosis-works.md)

**Blinding.** Keeping participants, clinicians or raters unaware of who received which treatment, so expectations cannot sway the results. A "probably blinded" rating is one made by someone unlikely to know, such as a teacher not told about a trial. → [Neurofeedback, diet, supplements, exercise: do they help ADHD?](../06-treatment-what-works/lifestyle-and-other-approaches.md)

## C

**Candidate gene.** A single gene studied because it was suspected to matter, such as the dopamine transporter gene (DAT1) or the dopamine D4 receptor gene (DRD4). Genome-wide studies have largely replaced this approach, and these genes have small effects. → [Is ADHD genetic, and will my children have it?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/genes-and-inheritance.md)

**Case-control study.** A study that starts with people who have an outcome (cases) and compares their past exposures with people who do not (controls). Useful for rare outcomes, but prone to bias in how cases and controls are chosen. → [Reading ADHD research: what do the numbers really mean?](how-to-read-the-evidence.md)

**Catecholamines.** The chemical family that includes dopamine and noradrenaline, the main messengers that ADHD medicines act on. → [Is ADHD caused by low dopamine?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/dopamine-noradrenaline-and-myths.md)

**Certainty of evidence.** A rating of how confident reviewers are that a result is close to the truth, graded high, moderate, low or very low using a system called GRADE. Two groups can grade the same data differently, as Cochrane and the 2025 BMJ umbrella review did for methylphenidate. → [How well does ADHD medication work?](../06-treatment-what-works/how-well-medication-works.md)

**Chronotype.** A person's natural tendency to sleep and wake earlier or later. People with ADHD are, on average, later chronotypes ("night owls"). → [Sleep and ADHD: why can't I fall asleep, and is sleep the cause?](../03-why-i-think-and-feel-this-way/sleep-and-the-body-clock.md)

**Circadian rhythm.** The body's roughly 24-hour internal clock, which controls sleep timing, hormone release and alertness. → [Sleep and ADHD: why can't I fall asleep, and is sleep the cause?](../03-why-i-think-and-feel-this-way/sleep-and-the-body-clock.md)

**Classifier.** A machine-learning program trained to sort people into groups, such as "ADHD" and "no ADHD", from scans or other data. Classifiers tested on separate data have worked less well than early studies claimed. → [Does ADHD mean my brain is damaged or "three years behind"?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/brain-structure-and-development.md)

**Cochrane review.** A systematic review produced under the methods of Cochrane, an international research network known for strict appraisal of trial quality. Its reviews sometimes rate evidence lower than other groups do. → [How well does ADHD medication work?](../06-treatment-what-works/how-well-medication-works.md)

**Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT).** A structured talking therapy that works on thoughts, habits and practical skills. Adapted versions for adult ADHD focus on organisation, planning and procrastination. → [Therapy, coaching and skills training: do they work for ADHD?](../06-treatment-what-works/therapy-and-skills.md)

**Cohen's d.** A common effect size: the gap between two group averages in units of how spread out people are. See *effect size*. → [Does ADHD mean my brain is damaged or "three years behind"?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/brain-structure-and-development.md)

**Cohort study.** A study that follows a group of people over time and records what happens to them. A birth cohort follows people from birth, such as the Dunedin study in New Zealand. → [Diagnosed as an adult: how was my ADHD missed, and is it the same?](../01-is-this-real/diagnosed-as-an-adult.md)

**Confidence interval (95% CI).** The range the true figure probably lies in, given the data. A wide interval means the estimate is imprecise; one that includes no difference means the result could be chance. → [Why are girls and women with ADHD so often missed?](../01-is-this-real/women-and-girls.md)

**Confounding.** When a third factor drives both an exposure and an outcome, creating a link between them that is not causal. Shared family genes are a common confounder in ADHD research. → [What causes ADHD, and is it my fault or my parents'?](../04-did-i-cause-this-and-where-is-it-going/what-does-and-doesnt-cause-adhd.md)

**Confounding by indication.** A kind of confounding in which the reason someone is prescribed a medicine is itself linked to the outcome being studied. It is a central problem for registry studies of medication. → [Real-life outcomes on ADHD medication: what do registries show?](../06-treatment-what-works/what-registries-show.md)

**Consensus statement.** A document in which a group of experts sets out what they agree the evidence shows. Its strength depends on the evidence and the process behind it. → [Where does the information on these pages come from?](sources-and-guidelines.md)

**Co-occurring condition (comorbidity).** A second condition present at the same time, such as anxiety alongside ADHD. → [Anxiety and depression with ADHD: why are they so common?](../05-what-often-comes-with-it/anxiety-and-depression.md)

**Copy number variant (CNV).** A large, rare deletion or duplication of a stretch of DNA. CNVs are about twice as common in children with ADHD, but are found in only a minority. → [Is ADHD genetic, and will my children have it?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/genes-and-inheritance.md)

**Cortex.** The brain's thin, folded outer layer, involved in thinking, planning and perception. Studies measure its thickness and surface area. → [Does ADHD mean my brain is damaged or "three years behind"?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/brain-structure-and-development.md)

**Crossover trial.** A trial in which each participant receives both the treatment and the comparison, such as a placebo, in turn. Each person acts as their own comparison. → [Do periods, pregnancy and menopause make ADHD worse?](../04-did-i-cause-this-and-where-is-it-going/hormones-cycles-and-menopause.md)

**Cross-sectional study.** A study that measures each person once, at one point in time. It can show that two things go together but not which came first. → [Sleep and ADHD: why can't I fall asleep, and is sleep the cause?](../03-why-i-think-and-feel-this-way/sleep-and-the-body-clock.md)

## D

**DAT (dopamine transporter).** A protein that clears dopamine away after it is released by pulling it back into nerve cells. Methylphenidate and amphetamines block it. → [Is ADHD caused by low dopamine?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/dopamine-noradrenaline-and-myths.md)

**Default mode network.** A set of brain areas most active when the mind is at rest or wandering. Some studies find it more active during tasks in ADHD. → [Is ADHD caused by low dopamine?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/dopamine-noradrenaline-and-myths.md)

**Delay aversion.** A strong dislike of waiting. In the dual pathway model it is one of two partly separate routes into ADHD. → [Time and waiting in ADHD: why do I lose track and hate waiting?](../03-why-i-think-and-feel-this-way/time-waiting-and-rewards.md)

**Delay discounting.** The tendency to value a reward less the longer you have to wait for it. People with ADHD discount delayed rewards more steeply, on average. → [Time and waiting in ADHD: why do I lose track and hate waiting?](../03-why-i-think-and-feel-this-way/time-waiting-and-rewards.md)

**Diagnostic criteria.** The agreed list of symptoms, duration, age of onset and impairment that must be met for a diagnosis, set out in the DSM and ICD. → [How is ADHD diagnosed, and why is there no test?](../01-is-this-real/how-diagnosis-works.md)

**Diffusion tensor imaging (DTI).** An MRI method that tracks how water moves along white matter, the bundles of fibres that connect brain regions. It is used to study the brain's wiring. → [Does ADHD mean my brain is damaged or "three years behind"?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/brain-structure-and-development.md)

**Dimensional.** Describes a trait that varies by degree across everyone, rather than being simply present or absent. ADHD traits are dimensional; a diagnosis is a threshold on that dimension. → [What is ADHD, actually?](../01-is-this-real/what-adhd-is.md)

**DLMO (dim-light melatonin onset).** The time in the evening when the body starts releasing the hormone melatonin under dim light. It is the standard laboratory marker of body-clock timing, and it is later on average in ADHD. → [Sleep and ADHD: why can't I fall asleep, and is sleep the cause?](../03-why-i-think-and-feel-this-way/sleep-and-the-body-clock.md)

**Dopamine.** A brain chemical messenger involved in motivation, reward, movement and attention. It is one of the main targets of ADHD medicines, but "ADHD is low dopamine" is not an established fact. → [Is ADHD caused by low dopamine?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/dopamine-noradrenaline-and-myths.md)

**DSM-5-TR.** The *Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders*, 5th edition, text revision (2022), published by the American Psychiatric Association. It is used for diagnosis in the US, Australia and many other places. → [How is ADHD diagnosed, and why is there no test?](../01-is-this-real/how-diagnosis-works.md)

**Dual pathway model.** A theory that at least two partly separate routes lead to ADHD: one through executive difficulties and one through motivation, especially delay aversion. It is a theoretical model, not a tested finding. → [Why is my ADHD so different from someone else's?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/why-no-two-adhd-brains-are-alike.md)

## E

**EEG and QEEG.** EEG (electroencephalography) records the brain's electrical waves through the scalp; QEEG (quantitative EEG) converts them into numbers. Neither can diagnose ADHD. → [Is ADHD caused by low dopamine?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/dopamine-noradrenaline-and-myths.md)

**Effect size.** A measure of how big a difference is between groups, in units of how spread out people are (standard deviations). Common versions are Cohen's *d*, Hedges' *g* and the standardised mean difference (SMD); by convention 0.2 is small, 0.5 medium and 0.8 large. → [Reading ADHD research: what do the numbers really mean?](how-to-read-the-evidence.md)

**Emotional blunting.** Feeling flatter or less emotional than usual. How often it happens on ADHD medication in adults has not been measured. → [Will ADHD medication change my personality or make me a zombie?](../06-treatment-what-works/will-medication-change-me.md)

**Emotional lability.** Rapid, intense shifts of mood. On average ADHD medicines reduce it in adults, by about half as much as they reduce core symptoms. → [Emotions and rejection in ADHD: why do they hit so hard?](../03-why-i-think-and-feel-this-way/emotions-and-rejection.md)

**Emotion dysregulation.** Difficulty controlling how quickly, how strongly and for how long emotions are felt and expressed. It is common in ADHD but is not part of the diagnostic criteria. → [Emotions and rejection in ADHD: why do they hit so hard?](../03-why-i-think-and-feel-this-way/emotions-and-rejection.md)

**ENIGMA.** An international consortium that pools brain scans from many research sites to get large samples. Its ADHD working group produced the main studies on brain volume. → [Does ADHD mean my brain is damaged or "three years behind"?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/brain-structure-and-development.md)

**Executive function.** The mental skills used to plan, start, organise, switch between and stop tasks, including working memory, response inhibition and vigilance. → [Executive function and ADHD: why can't I plan or get started?](../03-why-i-think-and-feel-this-way/executive-function-and-working-memory.md)

## F

**Familial confounding.** Confounding caused by factors shared within families, such as genes or home environment. Sibling comparisons are designed to remove much of it. → [What causes ADHD, and is it my fault or my parents'?](../04-did-i-cause-this-and-where-is-it-going/what-does-and-doesnt-cause-adhd.md)

**fMRI (functional MRI).** An MRI method that tracks changes in blood oxygen, a stand-in for how hard a brain area is working. Task fMRI scans people during a test; resting-state fMRI scans them lying still. → [Is ADHD caused by low dopamine?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/dopamine-noradrenaline-and-myths.md)

**Functional connectivity.** Which brain areas rise and fall in activity together, usually measured with resting-state fMRI, used as a measure of how brain networks communicate. → [Is ADHD caused by low dopamine?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/dopamine-noradrenaline-and-myths.md)

## G

**Genome-wide association study (GWAS).** A study that scans markers across the whole genome in many people to find variants more common in those with a condition. Each variant found usually has a very small effect. → [Is ADHD genetic, and will my children have it?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/genes-and-inheritance.md)

**GRADE.** A system for judging certainty of evidence, which asks reviewers to mark evidence down for risk of bias, inconsistency between studies and other problems. See *certainty of evidence*. → [How well does ADHD medication work?](../06-treatment-what-works/how-well-medication-works.md)

**Grey matter and white matter.** Grey matter is the brain tissue made mostly of nerve cell bodies; white matter is the bundles of fibres that connect brain regions. → [Does ADHD mean my brain is damaged or "three years behind"?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/brain-structure-and-development.md)

**Guideline (clinical guideline).** Recommendations for clinicians, produced by a national or professional body after reviewing the evidence, such as NICE NG87 in the UK or the AADPA guideline in Australia. → [Where does the information on these pages come from?](sources-and-guidelines.md)

## H

**Hazard ratio (HR).** A relative measure of how often an event happens in one group compared with another at any given time, over a follow-up period. An HR of 2 means the event happens at about twice the rate; it does not tell you how common the event is. → [Reading ADHD research: what do the numbers really mean?](how-to-read-the-evidence.md)

**Hedges' g.** An effect size very like Cohen's *d*, with a small correction for sample size. See *effect size*. → [Emotions and rejection in ADHD: why do they hit so hard?](../03-why-i-think-and-feel-this-way/emotions-and-rejection.md)

**Heritability.** The share of the differences between people in a particular population that is linked to genetic differences between them. It does not describe one person, and it does not mean fixed or unchangeable. → [Is ADHD genetic, and will my children have it?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/genes-and-inheritance.md)

**Heterogeneity.** Variation, either between people with the same diagnosis or between the results of different studies. High heterogeneity in a meta-analysis means studies disagree more than chance would explain. → [Why is my ADHD so different from someone else's?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/why-no-two-adhd-brains-are-alike.md)

**Hippocampus.** A structure deep in the brain involved in memory. It is one of the subcortical regions measured in large ADHD scan studies. → [Does ADHD mean my brain is damaged or "three years behind"?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/brain-structure-and-development.md)

**Hyperfocus.** Periods of intense, hard-to-break absorption in an activity. Many people with ADHD report it, but it has been little studied and is not a diagnostic feature. → [ADHD and hyperfocus: why can I focus for hours on some things?](../03-why-i-think-and-feel-this-way/attention-regulation-and-hyperfocus.md)

## I

**ICD-11.** The *International Classification of Diseases*, 11th revision, from the World Health Organization. It is used for diagnosis and health records in the UK, Europe and much of the world. → [How is ADHD diagnosed, and why is there no test?](../01-is-this-real/how-diagnosis-works.md)

**Impairment.** Real-world difficulty caused by symptoms, at work, study, home, in relationships or in safety. A diagnosis of ADHD requires it. → [How is ADHD diagnosed, and why is there no test?](../01-is-this-real/how-diagnosis-works.md)

**Incidence (cumulative incidence).** Incidence counts new cases in a period; cumulative incidence is the estimated share of people who have had a first diagnosis by a given age. Prevalence, by contrast, counts everyone who has a condition at a given time. → [How is ADHD diagnosed, and why is there no test?](../01-is-this-real/how-diagnosis-works.md)

**Inverted U.** A relationship where moderate levels of something work best and too little or too much make things worse. Animal and human work suggests prefrontal function follows this pattern with dopamine and noradrenaline signalling. → [How do ADHD medicines work?](../06-treatment-what-works/how-adhd-medicines-work.md)

## L

**Late-onset ADHD.** ADHD that appears to start in adolescence or adulthood rather than childhood. When apparent late-onset cases were carefully reassessed, most were excluded, so whether true adult onset exists is debated. → [Diagnosed as an adult: how was my ADHD missed, and is it the same?](../01-is-this-real/diagnosed-as-an-adult.md)

## M

**Masking (camouflaging).** Hiding or compensating for difficulties in order to fit in. It is one reason ADHD can be missed, especially in girls and women, but there is no validated measure of ADHD masking. → [Why are girls and women with ADHD so often missed?](../01-is-this-real/women-and-girls.md)

**Mega-analysis.** Pooling the raw data from many studies, such as individual brain scans, and analysing it together, rather than combining their published results. → [Does ADHD mean my brain is damaged or "three years behind"?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/brain-structure-and-development.md)

**Mendelian randomisation.** A method that uses genetic variants as a natural experiment to test whether one condition may lead to another. → [Anxiety and depression with ADHD: why are they so common?](../05-what-often-comes-with-it/anxiety-and-depression.md)

**Meta-analysis.** A statistical method that combines the results of many studies on the same question into one overall estimate. A meta-analysis of small, unblinded trials is still small and unblinded. → [Reading ADHD research: what do the numbers really mean?](how-to-read-the-evidence.md)

**Meta-regression.** A meta-analysis that tests whether study features, such as year or method, explain differences between results. It showed that ADHD prevalence in children has not risen once methods are standardised. → [Why are ADHD diagnoses rising, and is it a fad?](../01-is-this-real/more-diagnoses-than-ever.md)

**Methylphenidate.** A stimulant medicine that blocks the dopamine and noradrenaline transporters, so these messengers stay active for longer. It is sold under brand names such as Ritalin and Concerta. → [How do ADHD medicines work?](../06-treatment-what-works/how-adhd-medicines-work.md)

**Minimal clinically important difference.** The smallest change on a scale that patients or clinicians would notice as meaningful, also called the smallest clinically relevant difference. → [How well does ADHD medication work?](../06-treatment-what-works/how-well-medication-works.md)

**Mortality rate ratio (MRR).** The death rate in one group divided by the death rate in another. Read it alongside the actual death rates. → [Will ADHD shorten my life? The hard statistics in context](../04-did-i-cause-this-and-where-is-it-going/the-hard-statistics-in-context.md)

**MRI (magnetic resonance imaging).** A scanner that uses magnetic fields, not radiation, to take pictures of the brain's size, shape and wiring. Structural MRI measures anatomy; fMRI measures activity. → [Does ADHD mean my brain is damaged or "three years behind"?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/brain-structure-and-development.md)

**MTA study.** The US Multimodal Treatment Study, which randomly assigned 579 children with ADHD to different treatments for 14 months and then followed them into adulthood. Later follow-up papers report on slightly different numbers of children, such as 558. → [ADHD medication for children: is it safe and worthwhile?](../07-family-work-and-other-people/children-and-medication.md)

## N

**Narrative review.** A review in which experts summarise research without a stated, repeatable search method. It is useful for ideas but more open to selective reading than a systematic review. → [Partner with ADHD: what does research say about relationships?](../07-family-work-and-other-people/when-your-partner-has-adhd.md)

**Natural experiment.** A situation in which something outside researchers' control, such as a school-entry cut-off date, sorts people into groups almost at random. It allows stronger conclusions than ordinary observational data. → [Why are ADHD diagnoses rising, and is it a fad?](../01-is-this-real/more-diagnoses-than-ever.md)

**Network meta-analysis.** A meta-analysis that compares several treatments at once, including pairs never tested against each other directly. → [How well does ADHD medication work?](../06-treatment-what-works/how-well-medication-works.md)

**Neurodevelopmental.** Describes conditions, such as ADHD and autism, that arise from differences in how the brain develops, usually becoming visible in childhood. → [What is ADHD, actually?](../01-is-this-real/what-adhd-is.md)

**Neurodiversity.** The view that ADHD and similar conditions are natural variation in how brains work, rather than only disorders. It can sit alongside a clinical view. → [Just diagnosed with ADHD: why do I feel relieved, sad and doubtful?](../01-is-this-real/after-the-diagnosis.md)

**Neurofeedback.** A training method in which people watch a live display of their brain activity and try to change it. Blinded trials have not shown it reduces ADHD symptoms. → [Neurofeedback, diet, supplements, exercise: do they help ADHD?](../06-treatment-what-works/lifestyle-and-other-approaches.md)

**Non-stimulant.** An ADHD medicine that is not a stimulant, such as atomoxetine, viloxazine, guanfacine or clonidine. Non-stimulants work more slowly or act differently. → [How do ADHD medicines work?](../06-treatment-what-works/how-adhd-medicines-work.md)

**Noradrenaline (norepinephrine).** A brain chemical messenger involved in alertness and attention. Many ADHD medicines act on it. → [Is ADHD caused by low dopamine?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/dopamine-noradrenaline-and-myths.md)

**Normative modelling.** Building a reference chart of typical brain measures for each age, like a height-for-age growth chart, then asking where each individual sits on it. It shows that people with ADHD differ from typical in their own individual ways. → [Why is my ADHD so different from someone else's?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/why-no-two-adhd-brains-are-alike.md)

## O

**Observational study.** A study that records what happens without assigning treatment. It can show associations but not prove cause. → [Reading ADHD research: what do the numbers really mean?](how-to-read-the-evidence.md)

**Odds ratio (OR).** A relative measure comparing the odds of an outcome in two groups. When outcomes are rare it is close to the relative risk. → [Reading ADHD research: what do the numbers really mean?](how-to-read-the-evidence.md)

**Overlap.** How much two groups' scores fall in the same range. Small effect sizes mean large overlap: at an effect size of 0.2, about 92%. → [Reading ADHD research: what do the numbers really mean?](how-to-read-the-evidence.md)

## P

**Person-years.** One person followed for one year. Rates per person-year allow fair comparisons when people are followed for different lengths of time. → [Will ADHD shorten my life? The hard statistics in context](../04-did-i-cause-this-and-where-is-it-going/the-hard-statistics-in-context.md)

**PET and SPECT.** Brain-scanning methods that use small amounts of a radioactive tracer to measure molecules in the living brain, such as the dopamine transporter. They are research tools, not diagnostic tests for ADHD. → [Is ADHD caused by low dopamine?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/dopamine-noradrenaline-and-myths.md)

**Placebo.** An inactive treatment that looks the same as the real one, used so that expectation effects can be measured. → [How well does ADHD medication work?](../06-treatment-what-works/how-well-medication-works.md)

**Polygenic.** Influenced by many genes, each with a tiny effect. ADHD is polygenic. → [Is ADHD genetic, and will my children have it?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/genes-and-inheritance.md)

**Polygenic score.** A number that adds up a person's many small genetic risks. In ADHD it differs between groups on average but cannot diagnose or predict for an individual. → [Is ADHD genetic, and will my children have it?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/genes-and-inheritance.md)

**Prefrontal cortex.** The front part of the cortex, important for planning, attention and self-control. → [How do ADHD medicines work?](../06-treatment-what-works/how-adhd-medicines-work.md)

**Premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD).** A severe form of premenstrual mood change. One clinic survey found many women with ADHD reported symptoms suggestive of it, but the evidence is thin. → [Do periods, pregnancy and menopause make ADHD worse?](../04-did-i-cause-this-and-where-is-it-going/hormones-cycles-and-menopause.md)

**Presentation.** The pattern of ADHD symptoms at a given time: mainly inattentive, mainly hyperactive-impulsive, or combined. A person's presentation can change over time. → [What is ADHD, actually?](../01-is-this-real/what-adhd-is.md)

**Prevalence.** The share of a population with a condition at a given time. A prevalence ratio compares that share in two groups. → [What is ADHD, actually?](../01-is-this-real/what-adhd-is.md)

**Psychoeducation.** Structured information about a condition and how to manage it, given to patients or families. → [Therapy, coaching and skills training: do they work for ADHD?](../06-treatment-what-works/therapy-and-skills.md)

**Publication bias.** The tendency for studies with positive results to be published more often, which can make treatments look better than they are. → [Neurofeedback, diet, supplements, exercise: do they help ADHD?](../06-treatment-what-works/lifestyle-and-other-approaches.md)

## Q

**Qualitative study.** A study, often based on interviews or focus groups, that describes what experiences are like rather than counting how many people have them. → [Just diagnosed with ADHD: why do I feel relieved, sad and doubtful?](../01-is-this-real/after-the-diagnosis.md)

## R

**Randomised controlled trial (RCT).** A study that assigns people by chance to a treatment or a comparison, so that the groups differ only by the treatment. It is the strongest design for testing whether a treatment works. → [How well does ADHD medication work?](../06-treatment-what-works/how-well-medication-works.md)

**Randomised discontinuation trial (withdrawal trial).** A trial in which people already doing well on a medicine are randomly assigned either to keep taking it or to switch to an identical-looking placebo, without knowing which. → [Stopping ADHD medication: do benefits fade, and is it for life?](../06-treatment-what-works/staying-on-or-stopping-medication.md)

**Reaction-time variability.** How much a person's response speed varies from one trial of a test to the next. It is among the largest and most consistent test differences in ADHD, but it is not unique to ADHD. → [ADHD and hyperfocus: why can I focus for hours on some things?](../03-why-i-think-and-feel-this-way/attention-regulation-and-hyperfocus.md)

**Registry study.** A study using national records, such as Swedish or Danish health and prescription registers, that cover whole populations. Registries include only diagnosed people and cannot prove cause on their own. → [Real-life outcomes on ADHD medication: what do registries show?](../06-treatment-what-works/what-registries-show.md)

**Rejection sensitive dysphoria (RSD).** A popular term for intense emotional pain after perceived rejection. It is not in the DSM-5-TR or ICD-11, and no validated questionnaire for RSD and no systematic review of RSD in ADHD was found. → [Emotions and rejection in ADHD: why do they hit so hard?](../03-why-i-think-and-feel-this-way/emotions-and-rejection.md)

**Rejection sensitivity.** An established psychology concept: a tendency to anxiously expect, readily perceive and react strongly to rejection. It is studied across many conditions, and is different from RSD. → [Emotions and rejection in ADHD: why do they hit so hard?](../03-why-i-think-and-feel-this-way/emotions-and-rejection.md)

**Relative age effect.** The finding that the youngest children in a school year are diagnosed with ADHD more often than the oldest. → [Why are ADHD diagnoses rising, and is it a fad?](../01-is-this-real/more-diagnoses-than-ever.md)

**Relative risk.** How many times more (or less) likely an outcome is in one group than another. Always read it alongside absolute risk. → [Will ADHD shorten my life? The hard statistics in context](../04-did-i-cause-this-and-where-is-it-going/the-hard-statistics-in-context.md)

**Remission and persistence.** Remission means no longer meeting diagnostic criteria; persistence means continuing to meet them. Partial remission means still having some impairing symptoms, but below the full threshold. → [ADHD over a lifetime: will it get better or worse as I age?](../04-did-i-cause-this-and-where-is-it-going/how-adhd-changes-over-life.md)

**Response inhibition.** Stopping an action that has already started. It is one of the executive functions on which people with ADHD score lower on average. → [Executive function and ADHD: why can't I plan or get started?](../03-why-i-think-and-feel-this-way/executive-function-and-working-memory.md)

## S

**Scoping review.** A review that maps what research exists on a topic, usually without judging study quality or pooling results. → [Emotions and rejection in ADHD: why do they hit so hard?](../03-why-i-think-and-feel-this-way/emotions-and-rejection.md)

**Screening tool.** A short questionnaire that identifies who might have a condition and should be assessed. It does not diagnose. → [How is ADHD diagnosed, and why is there no test?](../01-is-this-real/how-diagnosis-works.md)

**Sensitivity and specificity.** Sensitivity is how many people with a condition a test correctly picks up; specificity is how many people without it the test correctly clears. An American Psychiatric Association work group proposed that a diagnostic imaging test should reach at least 80% on both. → [Does ADHD mean my brain is damaged or "three years behind"?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/brain-structure-and-development.md)

**Sibling comparison (sibling-control design).** A study design comparing brothers and sisters who differ in an exposure, which removes much of the confounding from shared genes and family environment. → [What causes ADHD, and is it my fault or my parents'?](../04-did-i-cause-this-and-where-is-it-going/what-does-and-doesnt-cause-adhd.md)

**SNP heritability.** The share of differences between people explained by common genetic variants measured directly in DNA, as in a GWAS. It is much lower than twin heritability, a gap sometimes called "missing heritability". → [Is ADHD genetic, and will my children have it?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/genes-and-inheritance.md)

**Standard deviation.** A measure of how spread out people's scores are around the average. Effect sizes are expressed in standard deviations. → [Reading ADHD research: what do the numbers really mean?](how-to-read-the-evidence.md)

**Standardised mean difference (SMD).** An effect size used to pool trials that measured the same thing on different scales. See *effect size*. → [How well does ADHD medication work?](../06-treatment-what-works/how-well-medication-works.md)

**Statistically significant.** Unlikely to be due to chance alone, by a conventional threshold. It says nothing about whether a difference is large or matters. → [Reading ADHD research: what do the numbers really mean?](how-to-read-the-evidence.md)

**Stimulants.** The main group of ADHD medicines, methylphenidate and amphetamines, which increase dopamine and noradrenaline signalling. They act on the day they are taken and wear off within hours. → [How do ADHD medicines work?](../06-treatment-what-works/how-adhd-medicines-work.md)

**Striatum.** A group of structures deep in the brain, including the caudate, putamen and nucleus accumbens, involved in reward and action. → [Is ADHD caused by low dopamine?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/dopamine-noradrenaline-and-myths.md)

**Subcortical.** Below the cortex. Subcortical structures include the striatum, amygdala and hippocampus. → [Does ADHD mean my brain is damaged or "three years behind"?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/brain-structure-and-development.md)

**Systematic review.** A review that searches for all relevant studies using a stated, repeatable method, and appraises their quality. → [Reading ADHD research: what do the numbers really mean?](how-to-read-the-evidence.md)

## T

**Theta/beta ratio.** An EEG measure comparing slower theta brain waves with faster beta waves. The difference between ADHD and comparison groups has shrunk in newer studies, and it is not a diagnostic test. → [Is ADHD caused by low dopamine?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/dopamine-noradrenaline-and-myths.md)

**Time blindness.** A popular phrase for difficulty sensing time. Research finds time perception is less accurate and less precise in ADHD on average, not absent. → [Time and waiting in ADHD: why do I lose track and hate waiting?](../03-why-i-think-and-feel-this-way/time-waiting-and-rewards.md)

**Twin study.** A study comparing identical and non-identical twins to estimate how much of the variation in a trait is linked to genes. → [Is ADHD genetic, and will my children have it?](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/genes-and-inheritance.md)

## U

**Umbrella review.** A review of existing systematic reviews and meta-analyses, used to summarise a whole field, such as the 2025 BMJ review of ADHD treatments. → [Where does the information on these pages come from?](sources-and-guidelines.md)

**Unblinded rating.** A rating made by someone who knows which treatment a person received, such as a parent in a diet trial. Unblinded ratings tend to overstate benefits. → [Parent training and school support for ADHD: do they help?](../07-family-work-and-other-people/parenting-and-what-helps.md)

## V

**Vigilance.** Keeping attention on a long, dull or unrewarding task. It is among the more consistent test weaknesses in ADHD. → [ADHD and hyperfocus: why can I focus for hours on some things?](../03-why-i-think-and-feel-this-way/attention-regulation-and-hyperfocus.md)

**Viloxazine.** A non-stimulant medicine that blocks the noradrenaline transporter more moderately and acts on some serotonin receptors. Its extended-release form is approved for ADHD in the United States; no UK or EU licence for ADHD was found. → [How do ADHD medicines work?](../06-treatment-what-works/how-adhd-medicines-work.md)

## W

**Within-person design (self-controlled design).** A study comparing the same people with themselves at different times, for example during months on and off medication. It removes stable differences between people but not changes that happen at the same time as treatment. → [Real-life outcomes on ADHD medication: what do registries show?](../06-treatment-what-works/what-registries-show.md)

**Working memory.** The ability to hold and use information in mind over a few seconds, like a phone number while you dial it. → [Executive function and ADHD: why can't I plan or get started?](../03-why-i-think-and-feel-this-way/executive-function-and-working-memory.md)
