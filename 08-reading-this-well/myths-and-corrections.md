# ADHD myths: which popular claims are wrong or half right?

> **Short answer:** Many popular claims about ADHD stretch a real finding too far, and some are simply unsupported. A small group difference becomes "the ADHD brain"; a modelled estimate becomes a fact about life expectancy. Each claim below has a short correction and a link to the page that holds the numbers.

This page mentions claims about early death and dementia. The short answer above is the main point.

## About whether ADHD is real

**"ADHD is a fad, or overdiagnosed."**

Partly true, depending on where you look. When the same diagnostic methods are used, the share of children with ADHD has not risen over three decades, though diagnoses have [1], and there is evidence of milder cases being included and of the youngest children in a class being diagnosed more often. Some places, such as English primary care, still diagnose fewer adults than expected, so the fairest summary is rising recognition plus real noise in how diagnosis happens.

→ [More diagnoses than ever](../01-is-this-real/more-diagnoses-than-ever.md)

**"Everyone's a little bit ADHD."**

ADHD traits do vary across everyone, like blood pressure. A diagnosis marks the point where those traits cause lasting problems in daily life. A trait being common does not make the condition imaginary, in the same way that everyone having a blood pressure does not make high blood pressure imaginary.

→ [What ADHD is](../01-is-this-real/what-adhd-is.md)

**"It's only boys" / "Girls don't get it."**

Girls and women are diagnosed less often and later, and the gap between the sexes narrows in adulthood. Expert consensus puts much of this down to under-recognition rather than true absence.

→ [Women and girls](../01-is-this-real/women-and-girls.md)

**"An online quiz or TikTok video told me I have it."**

Screening questionnaires flag people who may need assessment; they cannot diagnose. A study of the 100 most popular ADHD videos on TikTok found about half (52) were misleading [2].

→ [How diagnosis works](../01-is-this-real/how-diagnosis-works.md)

**"You can't get ADHD as an adult" / "Adult-onset ADHD is a separate condition."**

Neither is settled. Some studies found many adults with ADHD had no childhood diagnosis; closer re-checking found most of these had earlier symptoms that were missed, or another explanation. Whether ADHD can truly begin in adulthood is still open, and that question does not decide whether an adult's difficulties are real.

→ [Diagnosed as an adult](../01-is-this-real/diagnosed-as-an-adult.md)

## About the brain

**"ADHD is a dopamine deficiency."**

Dopamine and noradrenaline systems are involved, and ADHD medicines act on them. Brain-scan studies of dopamine give inconsistent results, there is no test of a "dopamine level", and genetics points to many pathways. "Low dopamine" is a simplification, not an established cause.

→ [Dopamine, noradrenaline and myths](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/dopamine-noradrenaline-and-myths.md)

**"ADHD brains are three years behind" / "30% behind."**

The "three years" comes from one study of children that found a cortical thickness measure peaked about three years later, on average, in the ADHD group. It is not a measure of maturity, intelligence or worth, and the differences mostly fade by adulthood. The "30%" figure is a rule of thumb from talks, with no peer-reviewed source found.

→ [Brain structure and development](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/brain-structure-and-development.md)

**"You can see ADHD on a brain scan" / "The ADHD brain is smaller."**

Group differences in brain size are very small, and people with and without ADHD overlap by more than 90% on these measures. A review of 96 imaging studies found no single region consistently different. No scan can diagnose ADHD.

→ [Brain structure and development](../02-whats-happening-in-my-brain/brain-structure-and-development.md)

**"If a stimulant helps you focus, you must have ADHD."**

Stimulants sharpen attention in many people without ADHD too, so a good response does not confirm a diagnosis. In a small 1980 double-blind study, a single dose of dextroamphetamine reduced activity and improved vigilance and learning in boys without ADHD, and acted similarly in boys with ADHD and in young men [3].

→ [How ADHD medicines work](../06-treatment-what-works/how-adhd-medicines-work.md)

## About thinking and feeling

**"Rejection sensitive dysphoria is a core ADHD symptom."**

Strong emotional reactions are common in ADHD and well documented. "Rejection sensitive dysphoria" (RSD) is a term from clinical practice that is not in any diagnostic manual, and no validated questionnaire and no systematic review of RSD in ADHD was found (a 2026 scoping review covers RSD in autistic adults). The experience it names is real for many people, but whether it is part of ADHD itself is unknown.

→ [Emotions and rejection](../03-why-i-think-and-feel-this-way/emotions-and-rejection.md)

**"Hyperfocus is the ADHD superpower."**

Many people with ADHD describe periods of intense absorption, but the research is a few small self-report studies. Hyperfocus is not a diagnostic feature, and it happens in people without ADHD too.

→ [Attention regulation and hyperfocus](../03-why-i-think-and-feel-this-way/attention-regulation-and-hyperfocus.md)

**"Time blindness."**

On average, people with ADHD judge and estimate time less accurately, and the differences are real at group level. "Blindness" is a metaphor; it overstates how large the differences are.

→ [Time, waiting and rewards](../03-why-i-think-and-feel-this-way/time-waiting-and-rewards.md)

## About causes

**"Bad parenting causes ADHD."**

On current evidence, parenting style does not appear to cause ADHD, which arises mostly from many small inherited genetic differences. Adoption research suggests the arrow often runs the other way: a child's ADHD traits can draw out harsher parenting. Parenting can shape how things go, not whether ADHD is there.

→ [What does and doesn't cause ADHD](../04-did-i-cause-this-and-where-is-it-going/what-does-and-doesnt-cause-adhd.md)

**"Sugar, screens, paracetamol or smoking in pregnancy cause ADHD."**

Double-blind trials have not found that sugar changes children's behaviour [4]. The links with paracetamol and smoking in pregnancy largely disappear when siblings are compared, which points to shared family factors. Screens are linked with ADHD symptoms, but which causes which is unclear.

→ [What does and doesn't cause ADHD](../04-did-i-cause-this-and-where-is-it-going/what-does-and-doesnt-cause-adhd.md)

## About the future

**"People with ADHD die 13 years earlier."**

This figure is a model-based estimate from a life-expectancy calculator, applied to one Milwaukee study group: 131 young adults diagnosed with hyperactivity as children, and 71 comparison young adults [5]. The 12.7 fewer years applied to those whose ADHD persisted into adulthood; for the childhood-diagnosed group as a whole the estimate was 8.4 years [5].

It is not a count of deaths. Studies of actual deaths among diagnosed adults find a smaller and still uncertain gap, much of it from accidents and other risks that can change.

→ [The hard statistics in context](../04-did-i-cause-this-and-where-is-it-going/the-hard-statistics-in-context.md)

**"ADHD causes dementia."**

Several large studies of health records, from Israel and Sweden among others, find that people with ADHD are diagnosed with dementia more often later in life. The link is much weaker than headlines suggest: in the largest study it weakened considerably once other psychiatric conditions were taken into account, a systematic review found the studies too varied to combine, and early dementia can be mistaken for late-appearing ADHD. None of this shows that ADHD causes dementia.

→ [The hard statistics in context](../04-did-i-cause-this-and-where-is-it-going/the-hard-statistics-in-context.md)

**"ADHD always gets worse at menopause."**

Many women report this, and hormone effects are plausible, but research on menopause itself has only just begun. Two 2025 surveys compared women with and without ADHD in midlife and reached different answers, and neither followed the same women through the transition.

→ [Hormones, cycles and menopause](../04-did-i-cause-this-and-where-is-it-going/hormones-cycles-and-menopause.md)

## About treatment

**"Stimulants lead to addiction."**

Studies that compare people with themselves on and off medication find fewer substance-related problems during medicated periods; in US insurance data, the odds of a substance-related event were about a third lower in medicated months [6]. Misuse and passing medicine to others are real, separate problems.

→ [Alcohol, drugs and addiction](../05-what-often-comes-with-it/alcohol-drugs-and-addiction.md)

**"ADHD medication doesn't really work. Cochrane said so."**

Major reviews, Cochrane included, find similar short-term benefits. They disagree about how confident to be in that evidence, not about the size of the effect. Evidence for long-term benefit is weak for every ADHD treatment, not only medication.

→ [How well medication works](../06-treatment-what-works/how-well-medication-works.md)

**"Medication turns you into a zombie" / "It changes who you are."**

On average, emotional ups and downs become smaller on medication. Whether some people also feel flatter, and how many, has not been measured in adults; some people do report it, and children's trials found more apathy with methylphenidate.

→ [Will medication change me?](../06-treatment-what-works/will-medication-change-me.md)

**"Neurofeedback, brain training or supplements fix ADHD."**

When symptom ratings are made by people who do not know who got the treatment, neurofeedback shows no meaningful benefit. Supplements such as omega-3 show small effects at most.

→ [Lifestyle and other approaches](../06-treatment-what-works/lifestyle-and-other-approaches.md)

**"Either ADHD is a disorder, or it's just a difference (neurodiversity)."**

These views are not mutually exclusive. Some researchers argue for combining a neurodiversity view with clinical care, because diagnosis and treatment help many people [7]. These pages present the evidence without choosing between them.

→ [What ADHD is](../01-is-this-real/what-adhd-is.md)

## What is uncertain or debated

Several corrections above are themselves provisional. Overdiagnosis, adult onset, and how much to trust the medication evidence are live debates among researchers, and the linked pages give both sides.

A claim being unsupported means the evidence was not found, not that it has been disproved. Rejection sensitivity and menopause are areas where good research may change the picture.

## What this means for you

If a claim has worried or reassured you, the linked page gives the numbers, who was studied, and what is still unknown. A claim that sounds definite about your brain, your genes or your lifespan is almost always a group finding stretched too far.
