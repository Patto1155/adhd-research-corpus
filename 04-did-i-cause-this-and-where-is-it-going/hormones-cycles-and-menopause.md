# Do periods, pregnancy and menopause make ADHD worse?

> **Short answer:** Many women with ADHD report that symptoms change across the menstrual cycle, after giving birth and around menopause, and the idea is biologically plausible. But the research is very thin. The best systematic review found 11 studies in total, mostly small, and called the evidence "largely suggestive". As of 2025, reviewers found no studies that directly tested ADHD symptoms during menopause, and almost nothing on how ADHD medicines work at different hormonal stages.

## How little evidence there is

This page is short on findings because the field is. To be specific about the gap:

- **The menstrual cycle and puberty:** 11 studies in the most thorough review to date, mostly small [1]. An earlier review reportedly found only 4. <!-- CHECK: the earlier 4-article review is mentioned in the scoping report without a citation -->
- **After giving birth:** one survey of 209 women with ADHD, with no comparison group [2].
- **Perimenopause and menopause:** no studies that directly measured ADHD during the menopause transition, according to reviews published in 2025 and 2026 [3, 4]. <!-- CHECK: the "no empirical studies" statement is quoted from a 2026 Drugs & Aging review citing an earlier systematic review; neither was read in full -->
- **Medication and hormones:** no trials of ADHD medicines in women with ADHD at menopause were found for this page.

Much of what circulates online about hormones and ADHD comes from clinical experience and personal accounts. Those are worth taking seriously as starting points for research. They are not yet evidence about how common the effect is or who it affects.

## What the evidence shows

**There is a plausible mechanism.** Oestrogen interacts with the dopamine and noradrenaline systems that ADHD medicines act on, which is why researchers expect hormonal changes to affect symptoms. <!-- CHECK: mechanism statement from general knowledge; Kooij 2025 review discusses it but text not read --> The evidence for this comes mostly from animal and general-population studies, not from people with ADHD.

**Cycle and puberty findings point the same way, weakly.** A 2025 systematic review of 11 studies concluded that evidence was "largely suggestive" of links between sex hormones and ADHD symptoms in females, mainly in puberty and across the menstrual cycle [1]. The studies were few and often small, and the authors called for research on other life stages, including menopause [1]. "Suggestive" here means consistent with a link but not enough to say how strong it is or who it affects.

**Mood symptoms around hormonal changes were common in one survey.** In a Dutch survey <!-- CHECK: country (Netherlands) recalled --> of 209 women with ADHD aged 18 to 71, about 45% reported symptoms suggestive of premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD, a severe form of premenstrual mood change) [2]. <!-- CHECK: 45% (95 of 209) from a secondary summary, not the abstract --> Among those who had given birth, 57.6% scored above a common screening cut-off for postnatal depression [2]. Women in the menopause transition reported more anxiety, low mood, sexual difficulties, hot flushes and physical complaints [2]. The study had no comparison group of women without ADHD, PMDD symptoms were recalled rather than tracked day by day, and postnatal symptoms were asked about years later, so these figures may overestimate.

**An expert group says hormonal transitions matter and treatment research is missing.** A 2025 review by a European special interest group on female ADHD concluded that hormonal transitions tend to worsen ADHD symptoms and mood, and that pharmacological research and tailored treatments are lacking [3]. This is an expert synthesis of a thin evidence base, not a new study.

**The menopause medication trials were not in people with ADHD.** A small 2015 trial gave lisdexamfetamine, an ADHD stimulant, to 32 women aged 45 to 60 *without* ADHD who had new problems with planning and memory at menopause [5]. Over four weeks, self-rated executive function and one memory test improved, and blood pressure and heart rate rose [5]. This says nothing directly about women who already have ADHD.

## What is uncertain or debated

- **Almost everything.** How often symptoms change with the cycle, by how much, and whether menopause makes ADHD itself worse or adds separate symptoms (poor sleep, low mood, "brain fog") that look similar.
- **Overlap with menopause symptoms.** Concentration and memory complaints are common in menopause in women without ADHD. Studies would need to separate the two, and none has.
- **Hormonal contraception and hormone therapy.** Their effects on ADHD symptoms have not been studied well enough to summarise here.
- **Who is studied.** Many ADHD studies exclude women over 50 or those already on medication [4]. Trans and gender-diverse people are almost absent from this research.

## What this means for you

If your symptoms seem to shift with your cycle, after pregnancy or in midlife, that experience is widely reported and fits what little research exists. The evidence cannot yet say how common it is or what helps, so claims that sound precise are ahead of the science. Any change to treatment around these stages is a decision to make with a prescriber.
