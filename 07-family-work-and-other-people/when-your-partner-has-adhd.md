# Partner with ADHD: what does research say about relationships?

> **Short answer:** Not as much as you might hope: research on adult couples where one partner has ADHD is thin, mostly small self-report studies and narrative reviews. On average it suggests more conflict, lower satisfaction and less stable relationships, and partners often see things differently. None of this predicts how any particular relationship will go.

## What the evidence shows

**The overall pattern is more conflict and less stability, on average.** A 2021 narrative review in the *Journal of Marital and Family Therapy* concluded that much research points to adults with ADHD having shorter-lived and more discordant romantic relationships [1]. The review drew on a small literature and was not a systematic review or meta-analysis, so it cannot give pooled effect sizes.

A 2024 umbrella review (a review of 125 earlier reviews) reached the same broad conclusion: adults with ADHD were more likely to divorce and reported less relationship satisfaction and more conflict [2]. It summarised the earlier reviews rather than pooling numbers, so it adds breadth rather than precision.

**Conflict behaviour differs in some couples.** In a laboratory study of young adult couples, those in which one partner had the combined presentation of ADHD (both inattentive and hyperactive-impulsive symptoms) showed more negative and less positive behaviour during a conflict-resolution task, and this went with lower relationship satisfaction [3]. Couples where the partner had the mainly inattentive presentation looked more like couples without ADHD [3]. The study was small and in college-age couples.

**Partners see things differently.** Adults with ADHD tend to rate their own social and relationship skills higher than their partners, friends or family rate them, a pattern researchers call *positive illusory bias* [1]. This may explain some couples' sense that they are describing different relationships. It is a group tendency; many adults with ADHD are accurate or self-critical.

**Women with ADHD may face more relationship difficulty.** The 2021 review reported that women with ADHD had more romantic relationship challenges than women without ADHD, while differences between men and women with ADHD were few [1]. Some studies suggest male partners rate a female partner's ADHD-related behaviour as more disruptive than female partners rate a male partner's [1]. This last finding comes from a secondary summary of the review, not the review itself.

The points in this section beyond the review's abstract were not checked against its full text.

**Violence in relationships is a documented risk, in both directions.** A 2023 meta-analysis of 14 studies (about 1.1 million people) concluded that people with ADHD are at higher risk of being involved in intimate partner violence and sexual violence, both as *victims* and as perpetrators [4]. For partner violence, the odds were about 1.8 times higher for being a victim (4 studies) and 2.5 times higher for being a perpetrator (6 studies) [4]. For sexual violence they were about 1.8 times higher for being a victim (6 studies) and 2.7 times for being a perpetrator (3 studies) [4].

Each estimate rests on only a few studies, and these are relative figures: the review did not give an absolute rate, so how many people are affected cannot be stated here. The umbrella review notes that some studies did not account for conduct problems or antisocial personality disorder, which may explain part of the link [2].

## What is uncertain or debated

**Almost everything about cause.** It is not clear how much of the relationship difficulty comes from ADHD symptoms themselves (forgetting, impulsive spending, uneven sharing of household tasks), from co-occurring anxiety, depression or substance use, or from the frustration and resentment that build up when symptoms are unrecognised. Studies rarely separate these.

**Whether treatment helps couples.** No randomised trial of couples therapy for ADHD was found for this page. One systematic review of couples therapy where a partner has ADHD was seen in searches but could not be checked, and it appears to rest on a handful of studies.

Whether ADHD medication improves relationship outcomes has not been well tested; trials find that medication reduces symptoms, while evidence on whether it improves quality of life is mixed; see [How well does ADHD medication work?](../06-treatment-what-works/how-well-medication-works.md)

**Who is studied.** Most research involves heterosexual couples, often students or clinic samples, in the US or Europe. Evidence for same-sex couples, older couples, and couples where both partners have ADHD is very limited.

**Popular advice runs ahead of evidence.** Books and online material describe patterns such as one partner becoming a "parent" to the other. These descriptions may fit many couples, but they come from clinical observation rather than measured research.

## What this means for you

If your relationship feels harder than others seem to, the research suggests that is a recognised pattern, not a failing of either of you. It also suggests that partners often perceive the same events quite differently, which is worth knowing when conversations stall.

The 2021 review recommends that clinicians work with the couple rather than only the person with ADHD [1], but that recommendation has not yet been tested in trials. The research does not tell you whether your relationship will last, and it cannot.
