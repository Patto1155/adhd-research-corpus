# My partner has ADHD. What does research say about relationships?

> **Short answer:** Not as much as you might hope. The research on adult couples where one partner has ADHD is thin: mostly small studies, self-report questionnaires and narrative reviews, with no large trials of couples treatment. What exists suggests, on average, more conflict, lower satisfaction and less stable relationships. Adults with ADHD also tend to rate their own relationship skills more positively than their partners do. None of this predicts how any particular relationship will go.

## What the evidence shows

**The overall pattern is more conflict and less stability, on average.** A 2021 narrative review in the *Journal of Marital and Family Therapy* concluded that much research points to adults with ADHD having shorter-lived and more discordant romantic relationships [1]. The review drew on a small literature and was not a systematic review or meta-analysis, so it cannot give pooled effect sizes.

**Conflict behaviour differs in some couples.** In a laboratory study of young adult couples, those in which one partner had the combined presentation of ADHD (both inattentive and hyperactive-impulsive symptoms) showed more negative and less positive behaviour during a conflict-resolution task, and this went with lower relationship satisfaction [2]. Couples where the partner had the mainly inattentive presentation looked more like couples without ADHD [2]. <!-- CHECK: Canu et al. citation details (year 2013 vs 2014, title, volume) not confirmed; finding is from search summaries --> The study was small and in college-age couples.

**Partners see things differently.** Adults with ADHD tend to rate their own social and relationship skills higher than their partners, friends or family rate them, a pattern researchers call *positive illusory bias* [1]. This may explain some couples' sense that they are describing different relationships. It is a group tendency; many adults with ADHD are accurate or self-critical.

**Women with ADHD may face more relationship difficulty.** The 2021 review reported that women with ADHD had more romantic relationship challenges than women without ADHD, while differences between men and women with ADHD were few [1]. Some studies suggest male partners rate a female partner's ADHD-related behaviour as more disruptive than female partners rate a male partner's [1]. <!-- CHECK: this gender-rating finding came from a secondary summary of the review -->

**Violence in relationships is a documented risk, in both directions.** A 2023 meta-analysis of 14 studies concluded that people with ADHD are at higher risk of being involved in intimate partner violence and sexual violence, both as *victims* and as perpetrators [3]. Each estimate rests on only a few studies, and the results varied widely between them. The odds ratios reported in secondary summaries could not be checked against the paper, so they are not quoted here. The studies did not give an absolute rate that could be stated here, and the authors stressed that most people with ADHD are never involved in such violence. If you are worried about your safety, see [not medical advice](../08-reading-this-well/not-medical-advice.md) for where to get help.

## What is uncertain or debated

**Almost everything about cause.** It is not clear how much of the relationship difficulty comes from ADHD symptoms themselves (forgetting, impulsive spending, uneven sharing of household tasks), from co-occurring anxiety, depression or substance use, or from the frustration and resentment that build up when symptoms are unrecognised. Studies rarely separate these.

**Whether treatment helps couples.** No randomised trial of couples therapy for ADHD was found for this page. One systematic review of couples therapy where a partner has ADHD was seen in searches but could not be checked, and it appears to rest on a handful of studies. Whether ADHD medication improves relationship outcomes has not been well tested; adult trials have found that medication reduces symptoms but did not improve self-rated quality of life ([how well medication works](../06-treatment-what-works/how-well-medication-works.md)). <!-- CHECK: the couples-therapy systematic review was seen only as a search summary (informahealthcare 10.1080/10522158.2023.2165585) and is not cited -->

**Who is studied.** Most research involves heterosexual couples, often students or clinic samples, in the US or Europe. Evidence for same-sex couples, older couples, and couples where both partners have ADHD is very limited.

**Popular advice runs ahead of evidence.** Books and online material describe patterns such as one partner becoming a "parent" to the other. These descriptions may fit many couples, but they come from clinical observation rather than measured research.

## What this means for you

If your relationship feels harder than others seem to, the research suggests that is a recognised pattern, not a failing of either of you. It also suggests that partners often perceive the same events quite differently, which is worth knowing when conversations stall. The 2021 review recommends that clinicians work with the couple rather than only the person with ADHD [1], but that recommendation has not yet been tested in trials. The research does not tell you whether your relationship will last, and it cannot.
